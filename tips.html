Healthy eating tips

Simple habits for healthier eating

Healthy eating is not about strict diets or expensive superfoods. It is about small, realistic habits you can repeat every day. Start with one tip below, practise it for a week, then add the next.

Four everyday habits

Small changes that make a big difference

🥗
Tip 01

Build balanced meals

A balanced plate gives your body steady energy and keeps you full for longer. Instead of counting every calorie, think about the proportions on your plate.

  • Fill half your plate with vegetables or salad.
  • Use a quarter for lean protein such as eggs, beans, fish or chicken.
  • Use the last quarter for whole grains such as brown rice, oats or wholegrain bread.
  • Add a small amount of healthy fat, such as olive oil, avocado or nuts.
💧
Tip 02

Drink enough water

Water supports concentration, digestion and energy levels. Mild thirst is often mistaken for hunger, so drinking regularly can also help you snack less.

  • Keep a refillable bottle with you at work, school or college.
  • Drink a glass of water with every meal.
  • Add slices of lemon, cucumber or mint for flavour without sugar.
  • Check the colour of your urine: pale yellow usually means you are drinking enough.
🍽️
Tip 03

Control portion sizes

Even healthy food can leave you uncomfortable if portions are too large. Simple visual guides help you serve the right amount without weighing everything.

  • Use your hand as a guide: a palm of protein, a fist of carbohydrates and two handfuls of vegetables.
  • Serve food onto a plate in the kitchen rather than eating from the packet.
  • Eat slowly and pause halfway through the meal to check how hungry you still are.
  • Save leftovers for tomorrow's lunch instead of finishing everything.
📅
Tip 04

Plan meals ahead

Planning is the easiest way to eat well on a budget. When you know what you are cooking, you waste less food, spend less money and rely less on takeaways.

  • Choose your meals for the week before you go shopping.
  • Write a shopping list and stick to it.
  • Cook grains and chop vegetables in batches to save time on busy days.
  • Plan at least one "leftovers night" to use up what is in the fridge.
Meal planning

Plan your week in five simple steps

Set aside about 30 minutes at the weekend. This short routine saves time, money and stress for the rest of the week.

  1. Check your kitchen

    Look in the fridge, freezer and cupboards. Plan meals around what needs using first.

  2. Pick your meals

    Choose three or four main recipes and repeat them. Cook once, eat twice.

  3. Write a list

    List only the ingredients you need, grouped by shop section to save time.

  4. Prep ahead

    Wash salad, chop vegetables and cook a batch of grains on the same day you shop.

  5. Review and adjust

    At the end of the week, note what worked and what was wasted, then plan again.
